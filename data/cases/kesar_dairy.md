# Kesar Dairy: Go Direct, or Keep the Distributors Happy?

*A fictional teaching case written for classroom use. All names and figures are invented.*

## The decision

In September 2026, Anjali Deshmukh, CEO of Kesar Dairy Cooperative, had to recommend to her board whether Kesar should scale its own milk-subscription app across Pune, or shut the pilot and stay with the distributor network that had built the brand over 30 years. The board meeting was in two weeks.

## Background

Kesar is a farmer-owned cooperative with 42,000 member farmers in Pune and Satara districts. It procures about 3.1 lakh litres of milk a day and sells packaged milk, curd and paneer under the Kesar brand. Revenue in FY2026 was ₹1,480 crore, with a net surplus of ₹37 crore, most of which was paid back to farmers as a bonus.

Kesar sells through 46 distributors who supply about 1,850 retailers. Distributors earn a margin of 8% and retailers 6%. The distributor network delivers before 6 a.m. every day, including monsoon days, and several distributors have worked with Kesar for two generations.

## The threat

Quick-commerce apps now deliver milk in under 15 minutes across Pune and take a commission of about 12% from brands. Two private dairy brands have launched their own subscription apps with morning delivery. Kesar's market share in Pune city fell from 31% in 2023 to 26% in 2026, while its share in smaller towns stayed flat.

## The pilot

In April 2026 Kesar launched a subscription app in the Kothrud and Baner areas. Customers order the night before and receive milk at their door by 7 a.m.

| Pilot metric (April to August 2026) | Value |
|---|---|
| Active subscribers after 5 months | 2,400 |
| Average daily volume per subscriber | 1.4 litres |
| Monthly churn | 9% |
| Delivery cost per litre (own riders) | ₹7.5 |
| Distribution cost per litre (distributor + retailer) | ₹4.2 |
| Price premium accepted by app customers | ₹2 per litre |
| Customer rating | 4.6 out of 5 |

The app gives Kesar something it has never had: data on who buys what, and when. Pilot customers also bought paneer and curd 2.3 times more often than the city average.

## The proposal

The digital team proposes scaling the app to all of Pune over 18 months, targeting 60,000 subscribers. This needs ₹3.2 crore to rebuild the app, and about ₹11 crore of working capital for riders, cold boxes and dark stores, about ₹14 crore in total. Delivery cost is expected to fall to ₹5.1 per litre at scale.

## The pushback

Three of the largest distributors, who together handle 22% of Kesar's Pune volume, have said they will give more shelf space to a rival brand if Kesar "competes with its own partners". Farmer representatives worry that money spent on an app is money not paid as a bonus. Elections to the cooperative's board are due in eight months.

## Questions for the board

Should Kesar scale the app, keep it as a small premium channel, or close it? What would Kesar need to believe for each option to be right?
