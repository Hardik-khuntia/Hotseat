# SkyLane Airways: The Tier-2 Route Bet

*A fictional teaching case written for classroom use. All names and figures are invented.*

## The decision

Vikram Rao, CEO of SkyLane Airways, had six new aircraft arriving between January and June 2027. He had to decide where to fly them. The network team wanted to open six routes from tier-2 cities that no other airline served directly. The finance team wanted to add frequencies on the busy metro routes where SkyLane already made money. The board would decide in October 2026.

## Background

SkyLane is a five-year-old low-cost airline with 28 aircraft, all A320s, flying 42 routes. FY2026 revenue was ₹6,900 crore with an operating margin of 4.1%. The two largest airlines together hold 78% of the domestic market; SkyLane holds 6%. Fuel is 38% of SkyLane's operating cost, and leasing is 17%.

## Option A: tier-2 routes

The network team proposes six routes connecting cities such as Hubballi, Jabalpur and Bareilly to Delhi, Mumbai and Bengaluru.

| Tier-2 routes (projected, year 1) | Value |
|---|---|
| Expected load factor | 68% |
| Break-even load factor | 74% |
| Average fare | ₹4,300 |
| Regional connectivity subsidy | covers 20 seats per flight for 3 years |
| Competition on these routes | none today |

With the subsidy, the network team expects these routes to break even in year 2 and to earn margins of 9% by year 4, because SkyLane would be the only airline on them and could build loyalty early. Airport charges at these cities are about 30% lower than at metros. However, three of the six airports have runways that limit A320s to 150 passengers in summer heat, against 186 seats normally.

## Option B: more metro frequency

The finance team proposes using the aircraft on existing metro routes.

| Metro routes (current) | Value |
|---|---|
| Load factor | 86% |
| Average fare | ₹5,600 |
| Operating margin | 7.5% |
| Share of seats on these routes held by the two largest airlines | 81% |

Adding flights would bring revenue quickly, but the two large airlines usually respond to new capacity by cutting fares on the same routes. In 2025, when SkyLane added flights on Delhi–Pune, average fares on that route fell 11% within three months.

## People and operations

SkyLane is short of 40 captains. Tier-2 routes would add night stops at smaller airports, which pilots dislike, and crew attrition was 14% last year. The chief of operations says fog in north India adds delay risk at two of the proposed airports from December to February.

## Investors

SkyLane's lead private equity investor owns 34% and wants an IPO within two years. It has told the board that "visible growth and stable margins" matter most to IPO investors.

## Questions for the board

Where should the six aircraft go: tier-2 routes, metro routes, or a mix? What risks would you accept, and what would make you change your mind?
