# Veloce Motors: Sell the Scooter Without the Battery?

*A fictional teaching case written for classroom use. All names and figures are invented.*

## The decision

Kabir Anand, founder of Veloce Motors, had to decide how to keep sales growing after the government halved its subsidy. The board would meet in October 2026.

## Background

Veloce, based in Pune, sells about 9,000 electric scooters a month and holds 4.2% of the electric two-wheeler market. Its scooter sells for ₹1.15 lakh, and the battery is 40% of its cost. In 2026 the subsidy per scooter fell from ₹10,000 to ₹5,000. In 2025 a battery fire led Veloce to recall 3,200 scooters.

## Option A: battery-as-a-service

A swapping company, ChargeHop, offers a partnership:

| Battery-as-a-service | Value |
|---|---|
| Scooter price without battery | ₹72,000 |
| Customer's monthly battery subscription | ₹2,200 |
| ChargeHop swap stations | 900, in 6 cities |
| Exclusivity required | 5 years |
| Veloce's share of subscription revenue | 10% |

## Option B: own battery plant

Build a battery-pack assembly plant for ₹380 crore. This would cut pack cost by 18% and give Veloce control over safety, but it would take 20 months.

## Option C: stay as is

Keep selling complete scooters and absorb some of the subsidy cut through lower margins.

## Other facts

Veloce's dealers earn more on complete scooters. Customers in surveys say "range anxiety" and "battery replacement cost" are their top two worries.

## Questions for the board

Which option should Veloce choose, and how should it manage the risks?
