# Jal Seva Foundation: Clean Water After the Grant

*A fictional teaching case written for classroom use. All names and figures are invented.*

## The decision

Meera Choudhary, Executive Director of Jal Seva Foundation, had to make the organisation financially sustainable. Her board would meet in October 2026.

## Background

Jal Seva runs 1,400 water ATMs in Rajasthan villages that sell purified water at ₹5 for 20 litres. About 2.1 lakh families use them. Running costs are ₹62 crore a year; 70% is covered by grants, mostly from one company's CSR fund, and 30% by water sales.

The CSR donor has said it will cut funding by 40% next year.

| Facts | Value |
|---|---|
| Average household income in these villages | ₹9,000 a month |
| Water bought per family | about 20 litres a day |
| Cost to Jal Seva per 20 litres | about ₹8 |
| Families who stopped using ATMs after a ₹1 price rise in 2024 | 11% |

## The options

1. **Raise the price** to ₹8 for 20 litres, covering costs.
2. **Cross-subsidy:** sell bottled water in nearby towns at a profit to fund village prices. Needs ₹18 crore to start.
3. **Hand over** ATMs to village councils (panchayats), with Jal Seva providing training and spare parts for a fee.

## Other facts

Health workers report fewer cases of waterborne illness in villages with ATMs. Some panchayats have managed similar systems well; others let them break down within two years.

## Questions for the board

How should Jal Seva stay financially sustainable without losing the families it serves?
