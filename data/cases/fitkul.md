# FitKul: Gyms, App, or Corporate Wellness?

*A fictional teaching case written for classroom use. All names and figures are invented.*

## The decision

Varun Chopra, founder of FitKul, had to decide where the company's money should go next. The board would meet in October 2026.

## Background

FitKul runs 85 gyms in 9 cities with 1.2 lakh members paying ₹2,100 a month. Gyms are only 48% full outside peak hours. 20 gyms lose about ₹40 lakh each a year.

Its app, with live and recorded classes, has 3.5 lakh downloads, but only 6% pay the ₹299 monthly subscription.

## Corporate wellness

FitKul has signed 4 companies for a corporate wellness pilot at ₹1,200 per employee per year, covering app access and gym visits. In the pilot, 34% of employees used the service at least once a month, and all 4 companies want to renew.

## The proposal

Close the 20 loss-making gyms and invest ₹60 crore in the app and a corporate sales team.

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Annual losses from 20 gyms | ₹8 crore |
| Members at those gyms | 22,000 |
| Expected members kept if moved to nearby gyms or the app | 55% |
| Corporate target in 3 years | 400 companies, 2 lakh employees |

## Other facts

Global fitness apps offer similar classes for less. Gym members say the best part of FitKul is its trainers. Leases on 8 of the 20 gyms run for 3 more years, with penalties for early exit.

## Questions for the board

What should FitKul do, and how should it balance gyms, the app and corporate clients?
