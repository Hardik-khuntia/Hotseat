# Tantu Apparel: Faster Fashion or Slower Craft?

*A fictional teaching case written for classroom use. All names and figures are invented.*

## The decision

Meera Rathore, CEO of Tantu Apparel in Jaipur, had to set the brand's direction for the next three years. The board would meet in October 2026.

## Background

Tantu sells ethnicwear through 140 stores and online. FY2026 revenue was ₹860 crore, with 22% from online sales. It launches 4 collections a year. Markdowns already cost 18% of revenue, and unsold inventory is worth ₹190 crore.

Customers under 25 make up only 19% of buyers. A fast-fashion rival launches about 40 new styles every week, and its prices are 30% lower.

## Option A: faster fashion

Move to two-week design cycles, with three times as many designs and smaller batches. This needs a new supply-chain system costing ₹45 crore and suppliers who can deliver in 15 days.

## Option B: certified handloom, premium

Tantu works with 6,000 weavers across Rajasthan and Gujarat. It could certify its products as handwoven and sustainably made, raise prices by 25%, and target buyers who care about craft. Market research suggests this segment is smaller but growing about 15% a year.

## Option C: fix inventory first

Spend a year cutting the number of designs, clearing old stock and improving forecasting before choosing a direction.

## Other facts

Weavers depend on Tantu for steady orders; a weavers' cooperative leader has warned that faster cycles would push work to power looms. Tantu's online customers are younger and buy 2.1 times more often than store customers.

## Questions for the board

Which direction should Tantu take, and what should it stop doing?
