# KhetSetu: Should a Farm Marketplace Start Lending?

*A fictional teaching case written for classroom use. All names and figures are invented.*

## The decision

Amit Tiwari, founder of KhetSetu, had to decide whether the company should scale up lending to farmers. The board would meet in October 2026.

## Background

KhetSetu is an app and network of village agents that sells seeds, fertiliser and pesticide to 2.6 lakh farmers in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar. Gross sales in FY2026 were ₹740 crore, and KhetSetu earns about 7% of that. In surveys, 38% of farmers said they would buy more if they could pay after harvest. Many currently borrow from moneylenders at 3% a month.

## The pilot

In 2025 KhetSetu partnered with a lending company (NBFC) to offer credit on inputs, repaid after harvest.

| Credit pilot (3 districts) | Value |
|---|---|
| Farmers who took credit | 14,000 |
| Purchases by credit farmers vs others | 1.8 times higher |
| Interest rate to farmers | 18% a year |
| KhetSetu's share of losses (first loss) | 5% of loans |
| Default rate, normal districts | 3% |
| Default rate, drought-hit district | 9% |

## The options

1. **Scale credit** to all districts, with KhetSetu's agents helping collect repayments.
2. **Stay a marketplace** and leave credit to banks.
3. **Slow pilot:** expand to 5 more districts with crop insurance bundled in.

## Other facts

Agents are trusted in their villages; using them to collect debts could change that. The regulator's digital lending rules require clear disclosure and no harsh recovery practices.

## Questions for the board

Should KhetSetu lend, and on what terms?
