# VidyaPath: Should an Online Coaching Brand Open Classrooms?

*A fictional teaching case written for classroom use. All names and figures are invented.*

## The decision

In September 2026, Ritu Saxena, CEO of VidyaPath, had to tell her board how the company would return to growth. VidyaPath prepares students for engineering and medical entrance exams, almost entirely online.

## Background

Revenue fell from ₹520 crore in FY2024 to ₹410 crore in FY2026, with a loss of ₹62 crore. VidyaPath has 3.8 lakh paid learners, but only 31% finish their course. Cash in the bank is ₹300 crore. Rivals have opened physical centres, and parents increasingly say they want "a classroom and a teacher who knows my child".

## The hybrid pilot

| Pilot (6 centres in Kota and Patna) | Value |
|---|---|
| Fee, hybrid course | ₹1.1 lakh a year |
| Fee, online course | ₹28,000 a year |
| Students per centre needed to break even | 220 |
| Average students per pilot centre | 180 |
| Renewal rate, hybrid | 64% |
| Renewal rate, online only | 22% |

## The options

1. **Own centres:** open 120 centres in two years at ₹1.6 crore each (₹192 crore in total), on five-year leases.
2. **Franchise:** let existing local institutes run VidyaPath-branded batches and keep 15% of fees, with no capital spent.
3. **Stay online:** cut marketing by 40% and focus on profitability.

## Pressures

Nine of the top 40 teachers left last year; the rest want a share of revenue from the courses they teach. VidyaPath's lead investor, who owns 28%, wants a clear path to profit within 18 months.

## Questions for the board

Which option should VidyaPath choose, and what evidence would make the board change course after a year?
