# Arogya Hospitals: Taking Specialist Care to Tier-3 Towns

*A fictional teaching case written for classroom use. All names and figures are invented.*

## The decision

Dr. Lakshmi Narayanan, Managing Director of Arogya Hospitals, had to decide how the group would grow beyond its home cities. The board would meet in October 2026.

## Background

Arogya runs 9 multispecialty hospitals in Tamil Nadu and Karnataka with 2,100 beds. FY2026 revenue was ₹1,950 crore with an EBITDA margin of 19%. Occupancy is 71%, and average revenue per occupied bed is ₹42,000 a day in its city hospitals.

Patients from smaller towns already travel 4 to 6 hours to Arogya's hospitals, often after their condition has worsened.

## Option A: build 12 spoke hospitals

| Tier-3 spoke hospitals (projected) | Value |
|---|---|
| Hospitals | 12, of 100 beds each |
| Cost per hospital | ₹45 crore |
| Total investment | ₹540 crore |
| Revenue per occupied bed | ₹18,000 a day |
| Break-even occupancy | 58%, expected in year 3 |
| Share of patients on the government insurance scheme | 55% |
| Scheme rates compared with cash rates | about 35% lower |
| Average delay in scheme payments | 110 days |

Spokes would link to the city hospitals for complex surgery, with scans read remotely.

## Option B: manage existing hospitals

Arogya would run 12 existing small hospitals owned by local doctors under operating contracts, earning 8% of their revenue as a fee, with no capital spent. Arogya would control protocols but not the buildings or equipment.

## Other facts

Specialists are reluctant to move to small towns; Arogya expects to pay a 30% salary premium. Two competing chains entered tier-2 cities in 2025.

## Questions for the board

Which route should Arogya take, and how should it protect quality and cash?
