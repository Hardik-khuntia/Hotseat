# Raftaar Logistics: Sell the Trucks?

*A fictional teaching case written for classroom use. All names and figures are invented.*

## The decision

Manpreet Gill, CEO of Raftaar Logistics in Delhi, had to decide the company's model for the next five years. The board would meet in October 2026.

## Background

Raftaar moves goods between warehouses and cities with 450 trucks it owns. FY2026 revenue was ₹690 crore with an operating margin of 5%. Trucks are used only 62% of the time, and diesel is 38% of costs.

## The proposal

Sell 300 trucks for about ₹110 crore and become a platform that matches loads with 12,000 small fleet owners through an app, earning about 9% of each load's value.

| Platform pilot (2 routes, 6 months) | Value |
|---|---|
| Cost per trip compared with own trucks | 16% lower |
| On-time delivery, own trucks | 94% |
| On-time delivery, platform trucks | 86% |
| Damage claims | up from 0.4% to 1.1% of loads |

## The client problem

Raftaar's largest client, an e-commerce company, provides 31% of revenue and requires 95% on-time delivery, with penalties below that.

## Other facts

Small truck owners often work long hours with little rest. Two freight apps backed by large investors already operate in north India, offering owners 7% commission.

## Questions for the board

Should Raftaar sell the trucks, keep them, or run a hybrid model? How would it protect its largest client?
