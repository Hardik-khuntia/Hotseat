# NetSathi: Wireless Now or Fibre for the Long Run?

*A fictional teaching case written for classroom use. All names and figures are invented.*

## The decision

Prakash Wagh, CEO of NetSathi, had to choose how the company would bring home internet to rural Maharashtra and Madhya Pradesh. The board would meet in October 2026.

## Background

NetSathi is a regional internet provider with 6.5 lakh subscribers. Average revenue per user (ARPU) is ₹420 a month and monthly churn is 2.8%. Its debt is 3.4 times EBITDA; its loan agreements require this to stay below 4 times.

## The options

| | Option A: fixed wireless | Option B: fibre |
|---|---|---|
| Investment | ₹260 crore | ₹610 crore |
| Villages covered | 1,800 | 600 |
| Time to roll out | 12 months | 4 years |
| Expected ARPU | ₹350 | ₹520 |
| Useful life of network | about 7 years | about 20 years |

Fixed wireless would use towers shared with a large mobile operator, paying it 18% of the revenue from those customers.

## The subsidy

A government rural connectivity programme pays 30% of fibre costs, but only for villages with more than 500 households. About 380 of the 600 planned fibre villages qualify.

## Other facts

A national mobile operator has started offering cheap home wireless in nearby towns. Village customers mainly want reliable video calls, online classes for children and government services.

## Questions for the board

Which option, or which mix, should NetSathi choose, and how should it stay within its loan limits?
