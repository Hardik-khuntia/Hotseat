# Glowveda: Quick Commerce, Pharmacies or Our Own Store?

*A fictional teaching case written for classroom use. All names and figures are invented.*

## The decision

Kritika Malhotra, founder of Glowveda, had to decide where to spend the company's ₹40 crore marketing budget next year. The board would meet in October 2026.

## Background

Glowveda sells Ayurvedic skincare products. FY2026 revenue was ₹180 crore, and the company is close to break-even.

| Channel | Share of revenue | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Marketplaces | 60% | heavy discounting |
| Own website | 25% | costs ₹780 to win a customer, who spends ₹1,900 in a year |
| Quick-commerce apps | 15% | growing 9% a month, but take 32% margin plus listing fees |

## The options

1. **Quick commerce:** move most of the budget to quick-commerce apps, paying for top placement.
2. **Offline:** sell through 4,000 pharmacies via distributors, who need a 25% margin and 60 days of credit.
3. **Own website:** keep investing in the website and a loyalty programme.

## Other facts

Quick-commerce apps have launched their own private-label beauty products. Customers who buy on the website return 3 times a year; marketplace customers mostly buy once, on discount. A larger beauty company has offered to buy a 30% stake in Glowveda.

## Questions for the board

Where should Glowveda spend, and how should it balance growth and profit?
