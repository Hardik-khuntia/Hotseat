# Rasayan Specialty Chemicals: The China+1 Plant

*A fictional teaching case written for classroom use. All names and figures are invented.*

## The decision

Hemant Patel, Managing Director of Rasayan Specialty Chemicals in Gujarat, had a contract offer from a global agrochemical company. He had to recommend whether to accept at the October 2026 board meeting.

## Background

Rasayan had FY2026 revenue of ₹2,250 crore and debt-to-equity of 0.4. Many global companies want suppliers outside China ("China+1") to reduce supply risk.

## The offer

| Supply contract | Value |
|---|---|
| Length | 7 years |
| New plant cost | ₹850 crore |
| Take-or-pay guarantee | 60% of plant capacity |
| Contract price | fixed for 3 years, then linked to raw material costs |
| Expected return if plant runs at 80% | 18% |
| Expected return at 60% (minimum guarantee) | 9% |

## Risks

Chinese prices for this chemical fell 30% in 2025 as Chinese plants cut prices to keep customers. The client could buy more from China if the price gap widens. The proposed site is in an industrial zone where the state pollution board has fined two neighbouring plants for effluent discharge; Rasayan would need a ₹70 crore zero-liquid-discharge system.

## Other facts

Rasayan has not built a plant this large before; its biggest so far cost ₹300 crore. Two Indian rivals are also bidding for China+1 contracts.

## Questions for the board

Should Rasayan sign, and on what terms?
