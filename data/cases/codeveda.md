# Codeveda: When AI Does the Work You Bill For

*A fictional teaching case written for classroom use. All names and figures are invented.*

## The decision

Arvind Raman, CEO of Codeveda in Bengaluru, had to set the company's response to AI coding tools. The board would meet in October 2026.

## Background

Codeveda employs 18,000 people and had FY2026 revenue of $620 million. 70% of revenue comes from time-and-material contracts, where clients pay for hours worked. Its top 5 clients provide 41% of revenue. Staff attrition is 17% a year.

## The problem

Internal tests show AI coding tools let teams deliver the same work with about 35% fewer hours. Two of the top 5 clients have asked for 20% price cuts at renewal, citing AI.

## The options

| | Option A: outcome-based pricing | Option B: protect hourly billing |
|---|---|---|
| What changes | Price by deliverables; use AI to cut hours | Keep billing hours; use AI quietly |
| Short-term revenue | down about 12% | flat, if clients accept |
| Margin | up from 18% to about 24% | flat at 18% |
| Investment | ₹140 crore to retrain 6,000 engineers | small |
| Risk | revenue dip, harder estimates | clients move to rivals who pass on AI savings |

## Other facts

Two larger rivals announced outcome-based contracts in 2026. Younger engineers are keen to learn AI tools; some senior managers are paid on team size.

## Questions for the board

Which option should Codeveda choose, and how should it handle clients and staff during the change?
