# Swarnam Jewellers: The Lab-Grown Diamond Question

*A fictional teaching case written for classroom use. All names and figures are invented.*

## The decision

Thomas Kurian, Managing Director of Swarnam Jewellers in Kerala, had to decide how to respond to lab-grown diamonds. The board would meet in October 2026.

## Background

Swarnam runs 62 stores in south India. FY2026 revenue was ₹5,600 crore, with gold 82% of sales and diamonds 15%. It promises to buy back natural diamonds at 90% of their current value, which customers trust.

## Lab-grown diamonds

Lab-grown diamonds look the same as natural ones but are made in factories. Their price per carat has fallen about 60% in two years and keeps falling.

| | Natural diamond jewellery | Lab-grown diamond jewellery |
|---|---|---|
| Swarnam's margin | 30% | about 45% today |
| Price trend | fairly stable | falling about 25% a year |
| Buyback promise | 90% of value | none planned |
| Customer interest, under 30 | 22% would consider | 61% would consider |

## The options

1. **Separate brand:** launch a new lab-grown brand with 40 stores, costing ₹220 crore.
2. **In existing stores:** sell lab-grown pieces in a separate section of Swarnam stores.
3. **Stay out:** focus on gold and natural diamonds.

## Other facts

Two national jewellery brands launched lab-grown lines in 2025. Some customers have complained elsewhere that they couldn't tell which diamonds were natural.

## Questions for the board

What should Swarnam do, and how should it protect customer trust?
