# SuryaGrid: Sell Solar Panels or Sell Solar Power?

*A fictional teaching case written for classroom use. All names and figures are invented.*

## The decision

Karthik Subramanian, CEO of SuryaGrid, had to decide whether to change the company's business model. The board would meet in October 2026.

## Background

SuryaGrid installs rooftop solar systems for small and medium factories (MSMEs) and sells them outright. FY2026 revenue was ₹420 crore with an operating margin of 9%. Many MSMEs want solar but can't afford the upfront cost.

## The proposal: sell power, not panels

SuryaGrid would own the panels on customers' roofs and sell them electricity under 20-year contracts.

| Power-sale model (plan) | Value |
|---|---|
| Capacity over 3 years | 150 MW |
| Investment per MW | ₹4.2 crore |
| Total investment | ₹630 crore |
| SuryaGrid's power price | ₹5.2 per unit |
| Grid electricity price for MSMEs | ₹8.4 per unit |
| Customer's saving | ₹3.2 per unit |
| Expected return | 14%, if payment defaults stay below 3% |
| Average delay in MSME payments today | 75 days |

## Risks

Two states changed their rules in 2025, reducing what businesses earn for power they send back to the grid. Some MSMEs close or move within 20 years. A climate fund has offered loans at 9.5% for up to 60% of the cost.

## Other facts

Competitors in the outright-sale business are cutting prices; installation margins have fallen from 13% to 9% in three years.

## Questions for the board

Should SuryaGrid make this switch, all at once or in stages, and how should it protect itself if customers don't pay?
